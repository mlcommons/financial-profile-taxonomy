# Crosswalk to Existing Frameworks

Profiles are designed as complements to existing frameworks: an agent assessment should be capable of discharging evidence obligations under several regimes at once.

| Profile element | AI risk-management frameworks | Risk-tiered AI legislation | Sector control catalogues | AI management-system standards |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Autonomy tier | Govern: oversight roles | Human-oversight obligations | Govern function | Management-system roles and oversight |
| Operational design domain | Map: context; Measure | Intended purpose; robustness obligations | Identify: asset and context | AI system impact assessment |
| Action class | Manage: risk treatment | Risk classification; high-risk annexes | Protect: entitlements | Annex controls |
| Control envelope | Govern and Manage | Risk management, logging, human oversight | Protect / Detect / third-party function | Operational controls |
| The Profile itself | Measure: the evidence the framework leaves unspecified | Logging and technical-documentation evidence | Detect / Respond | Documented information |

## Instruments in View

- **AI risk-management frameworks:** NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0, NIST AI 100-1) and its Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1)
- **Risk-tiered AI legislation:** EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) and its high-risk obligations
- **Sector control catalogues:** Cyber Risk Institute (CRI) Profile
- **AI management-system standards:** ISO/IEC 42001:2023

## Open Work

Control-level mappings (each [control](axes/control-envelope/README.md) to OWASP, NIST, and CRI controls) and jurisdiction-specific mappings, including to SR 26-2, which placed generative and agentic AI outside the scope of United States model-risk guidance and directed institutions to their broader risk-management practices, form part of the crosswalk deliverable and are open contributions.
