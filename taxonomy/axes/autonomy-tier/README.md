# Axis 1: Autonomy Tier (T)

## Definition

How much the agent decides and acts without a person, and who or what provides the fallback when the system cannot continue. The scale has five tiers, **T0 to T4**, plus a residual status, **U** (unprofiled). It is adapted from automated driving and the convergent structure of SAE J3016, the EASA AI Concept Paper, and related engineering taxonomies.

The tier determines what counts as a failure and how failures are weighted in evaluation.

**Value on a Profile:** one tier (or the status U), stated together with the six cross-cutting modifiers.

## Splitting Rule

Each tier is defined by two things: **what the agent does**, and **who or what provides the fallback** when the system cannot continue.

## Principles

**The tier attaches to the deployment.** Vendor certifications and vendor-asserted capability levels are insufficient on their own to classify a deployment. The institution's configuration choices are part of the classification.

**The tier is defined by evidence.** Configuration supplies the initial evidence; observed behavior confirms whether the asserted tier operates in practice. A deployment reaches T2 when the approval log shows that human approvals are substantive (reviewers take time, sometimes edit, sometimes reject), not merely because the configuration requires approval. Where logs show consistently reflexive acceptance, the deployment is classified by its effective operating posture, **T3, notwithstanding the software's configuration**.

**Unevidenced tier claims are treated as unprofiled (U).**

## Watchpoints

| Boundary | Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **T2 / T3** | Substantive review vs. reflexive approval | The boundary is behavioral. The human-factors literature (Bainbridge's ironies of automation; experimental work on automation complacency and bias; takeover-request performance in driving) documents that a person monitoring a competent automated system degrades as a fallback. Forced engagement tied to specific hazard and regulatory triggers may be needed to sustain a T2 designation over time. |
| **T3 / T4** | Human fallback vs. tested safe state | The question shifts from whether the person can intervene effectively to whether the system's safe-state design bounds the potential damage. A well-architected T4 deployment with tested safe-state behavior may be safer than a T3 deployment whose human fallback is unreliable. |

## Cross-Cutting Modifiers

Six modifiers are stated on the Profile alongside the tier and are material at any tier. They record risk dimensions the tier alone leaves unexpressed. On the action axis alone, a fully autonomous read-only agent is a lower-risk object than a copilot that drafts a wire transfer for one-click approval; the data-authority modifier can raise the risk of the read-only deployment materially, which is why the modifiers travel with the tier.

## Falsification

An autonomy-tier claim fails if the approval logs show reflexive acceptance.

## Assurance Note

The autonomy axis is the one on which the institution's own operational evidence remains indispensable even at the highest assurance levels, because distinguishing substantive from reflexive approval requires the approval logs of the actual deployment.
